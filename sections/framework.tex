\section{The coarsening framework, stated once}
\label{sec:framework}

We fix an abstract data-generating process that specializes to each
domain, state the coarsening-at-random conditions on it, and isolate
the object, the coarsening-sufficient statistic, that the consistency
theorem is about.

\subsection{Data-generating process}

A latent variable $\Y \in \mathcal{Y}$ is governed by a parameter
$\theta \in \Theta \subseteq \R^p$ through a density $g(y \mid
\theta)$. The analyst does not observe $\Y$. A coarsening mechanism
produces an observed report $R \in \mathcal{R}$, distributed as
$\kappa(r \mid y)$ conditional on the latent value. The observed
datum, possibly with covariates suppressed, is $R$; the analyst sees
$n$ independent reports $R_1, \ldots, R_n$ and wants $\theta$. The
\emph{candidate set} of a report is
\begin{equation}
  c(r) = \{ y \in \mathcal{Y} : \kappa(r \mid y) > 0 \},
  \label{eq:fw-candidate}
\end{equation}
the latent values the report does not exclude. In the discrete
reliability setting $c(r)$ is a finite set of components; in the
continuous additive-noise setting of differential privacy it is a
kernel-weighted continuum, the latent values $y$ reweighted by
$\kappa(r \mid y)$. The two are the same object at different
cardinalities, and we carry the weighted form throughout: the report
contributes the weight function $y \mapsto \kappa(r \mid y)$ to the
likelihood, and $c(r)$ is its support.

\subsection{The coarsening-at-random conditions}

The three conditions of \citet{towell2026masked} transcribe to the
abstract process directly. They are the masked-data specialization of
coarsening at random
\citep{heitjan1991ignorability,gill1997coarsening}.

\begin{condition}[C1: the candidate set contains the truth]
\label{cond:c1}
The report never excludes the latent value: $\Prob_\theta\{\Y \in
c(R)\} = 1$.
\end{condition}

\begin{condition}[C2: symmetric coarsening within the candidate set]
\label{cond:c2}
Conditional on the report $R = r$, the coarsening probability does not
depend on which admissible latent value is the true one: for all $y,
y' \in c(r)$,
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(r \mid y) = \kappa(r \mid y').
  \label{eq:fw-c2}
\end{equation}
A singleton candidate set, $|c(r)| = 1$, satisfies C2 vacuously.
\end{condition}

\begin{condition}[C3: coarsening independent of $\theta$]
\label{cond:c3}
The coarsening probability $\kappa(r \mid y)$, viewed for $y \in
c(r)$, does not depend on $\theta$.
\end{condition}

Under C1, the likelihood sum (or integral) over latent values
collapses to the candidate set. Under C2, the common coarsening weight
factors out of that sum. Under C3, the factored weight is a constant
in $\theta$ and drops from the optimization. The result is the
face-value likelihood: the analyst maximizes
\begin{equation}
  L(\theta) \;\propto\; \prod_{i=1}^n \int_{c(R_i)} g(y \mid \theta)
    \, d\nu(y),
  \label{eq:fw-likelihood}
\end{equation}
with $\nu$ the base measure on $\mathcal{Y}$ (counting in the discrete
case, Lebesgue in the continuous case), and the coarsening mechanism
itself is never modeled. This is exactly the C1--C2--C3 likelihood of
\citet[Theorem on the C1--C2--C3 likelihood]{towell2026masked}
specialized to a single coarse report per unit; the reliability paper
carries the additional censoring structure that we suppress here. The
ignorability that licenses \eqref{eq:fw-likelihood} is the standard
consequence of coarsening at random
\citep[Ch.~6]{little2002statistical}.

\subsection{The coarsening-sufficient statistic}
\label{sec:css}

The consistency theorem is not about $\theta$ directly; it is about a
statistic of the report that the coarsening preserves. Write the
face-value model in terms of a map from the latent law to the report
law. Define the \emph{coarsening-sufficient statistic} $\T :
\mathcal{R} \to \R^d$ as a statistic of the observed report whose
expectation under the fitted model is a smooth function of $\theta$
through the coarsened marginal, and which the model-implied mean
\begin{equation}
  m(\theta) \;=\; \E_\theta[\T(R)]
  \label{eq:fw-implied-mean}
\end{equation}
constrains the fit to reproduce. In a regular exponential family $\T$
is the family's sufficient statistic, and the face-value log-likelihood
\eqref{eq:fw-likelihood} then depends on the data only through the
empirical mean $\bar\T = n^{-1}\sum_i \T(R_i)$, so the fit reproduces
$\bar\T$ exactly (regime (A) of \cref{sec:consistency}). In a location
family $\T(R) = R$, and the likelihood depends on the full sample
through the score $\sum_i \psi(R_i - \mu)$ rather than through $\bar\T$
alone; the fit reproduces the sample $\psi$-location exactly and $\bar\T$
exactly only for a single report or for the Gaussian kernel, with an
$O_p(n^{-1/2})$ remainder otherwise (regime (B)). The
\citet{towell2026dpcoarsening} first-moment identity is the population
form of the location-family case.

The point of the synthesis is that each domain names a different $\T$,
and the consistency identity in each domain is the single equation
$m(\hat\theta) = \bar\T$ read in that domain's coordinates.
