\section{The general consistency theorem}
\label{sec:consistency}

We state the identity that the siblings rediscover under six names,
then recover each as a corollary. The identity is a stationarity
consequence: at an interior maximum of the face-value likelihood, the
fitted model must reproduce the empirical mean of the
coarsening-sufficient statistic. The cleanest hypothesis under which
this holds as an exact finite-sample equality is that the face-value
model is a regular exponential family; a second, structurally
different, hypothesis covers the continuous additive-noise case through
the location-family score, where the equality is exact for a single
report, holds as a first-moment identity in the population, and holds
in finite samples up to an $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ remainder.

\subsection{The theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Coarsened-data consistency at the MLE]
\label{thm:general-consistency}
Let the face-value model \eqref{eq:fw-likelihood} be maximized at an
interior point $\hat\theta$ of $\Theta$, and let $\T$ be the
coarsening-sufficient statistic of \cref{sec:css} with model-implied
mean $m(\theta) = \E_\theta[\T(R)]$. Suppose one of the following
regularity regimes holds.
\begin{enumerate}[(A)]
\item \textbf{Regular exponential family.} The coarsened report law
  $p(r \mid \theta)$ is a regular exponential family with natural
  sufficient statistic $\T(R)$ and natural parameter $\eta(\theta)$
  whose Jacobian $\partial\eta / \partial\theta$ has full column rank
  at $\hat\theta$. The Gaussian location family is the leading instance
  with $\T(R) = R$, so the additive-noise case with a Gaussian kernel
  falls here.
\item \textbf{Location family.} The coarsened report law is a location
  family in a scalar functional $\mu(\theta)$, that is, $p(r \mid
  \theta) = p_0(r - \mu(\theta))$ for a fixed symmetric, log-concave
  density $p_0$ with finite mean, and $\T(R) = R$.
\end{enumerate}
In regime (A) the fitted mean of the coarsening-sufficient statistic
equals its empirical mean as an exact finite-sample identity, in every
coordinate of $\T$,
\begin{equation}
  m(\hat\theta) \;=\; \bar\T,
  \qquad \bar\T = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n \T(R_i),
  \label{eq:general-consistency}
\end{equation}
up to optimization tolerance. In regime (B) the corresponding scalar
statement $\mu(\hat\theta) = \bar R$ holds in three precise senses: it
is exact for a single report ($n = 1$); it is the population
first-moment identity $\E_{\hat\theta,\kappa}[M] = m_{\mathrm{obs}}$ for
any $n$; and for $n > 1$ it holds in finite samples up to an error of
order $n^{-1/2}$, with $\sqrt{n}\,(\hat\mu - \bar R)$ asymptotically
normal. It is \emph{not} an exact finite-sample sample-mean identity
for $n \ge 3$ unless $p_0$ is Gaussian (in which case the report law is
already an exponential family and the exact identity is the regime-(A)
statement). What regime (B) reproduces exactly in finite samples is the
sample $\psi$-location, $m(\hat\theta) = \hat\mu$ with $\hat\mu$ solving
$\sum_i \psi(R_i - \hat\mu) = 0$, $\psi = -p_0'/p_0$; see
\cref{rem:loc-sketch}. In both regimes any bias in $\hat\theta$
relative to the true $\theta$ is absorbed into the part of the model
orthogonal to $\T$ (the coarsening-induced nuisance), and a check that
compares the fitted mean of $\T$ to its empirical value cannot detect
that bias.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Regime (A).} For a regular exponential family $p(r \mid \theta)
= h(r)\exp\{\eta(\theta)^\top \T(r) - A(\eta(\theta))\}$ the
face-value log-likelihood is $\ell(\theta) = \sum_i [\eta(\theta)^\top
\T(R_i) - A(\eta(\theta))] + \text{const}$. Its gradient in the
natural parameter is the mean-value identity for exponential families:
$\partial_\eta \ell = \sum_i \T(R_i) - n\,\nabla A(\eta) = n(\bar\T -
\E_\eta[\T])$, since $\nabla A(\eta) = \E_\eta[\T]$ is the standard
log-partition gradient \citep[Ch.~5]{vandervaart1998asymptotic}. By
the chain rule $\partial_\theta \ell = (\partial\eta/\partial\theta)^\top
\partial_\eta \ell$. At an interior maximum $\partial_\theta
\ell(\hat\theta) = 0$, and since $\partial\eta/\partial\theta$ has full
column rank, $\partial_\eta \ell = 0$, hence $\E_{\hat\theta}[\T] =
\bar\T$. This is \eqref{eq:general-consistency} coordinatewise, and
$m(\hat\theta) = \E_{\hat\theta}[\T]$ by definition.

\emph{Regime (B).} For a location family $p(r \mid \theta) = p_0(r -
\mu)$ with $\mu = \mu(\theta)$, the score in $\mu$ satisfies the
location identity $\partial_\mu \log p(r \mid \theta) = -p_0'(r -
\mu)/p_0(r - \mu) = -\partial_r \log p(r \mid \theta)$. The
face-value first-order condition in $\mu$ is $\sum_i \partial_\mu \log
p(R_i \mid \hat\theta) = 0$, equivalently $\sum_i \psi(R_i - \hat\mu) =
0$ for $\psi = -p_0'/p_0$. For $p_0$ symmetric and log-concave, $\psi$
is odd and nondecreasing, so $\sum_i \psi(R_i - \hat\mu)$ is strictly
decreasing in $\hat\mu$ and the first-order condition has a unique
interior root: $\hat\mu$ is the sample $\psi$-location, and the exact
finite-sample identity is $m(\hat\theta) = \hat\mu$. Since
$\T(R) = R$ and $\E[Z] = 0$ for the symmetric kernel,
$m(\theta) = \mu(\theta)$, so this reproduces $\hat\mu$ exactly. The
sample-mean form $\mu(\hat\theta) = \bar R$ requires $\hat\mu = \bar R$,
that is, $\psi$ linear; the only symmetric kernel with linear score is
the Gaussian, where the report law is an exponential family and the
exact identity is the regime-(A) statement. For a single report the
$\psi$-location is $R$ itself for any symmetric unimodal $p_0$, so
$\hat\mu = R$ exactly at $n = 1$. For $n > 1$ and a non-Gaussian
kernel, $\hat\mu$ and $\bar R$ are both consistent for the same
location, and standard $M$-estimator theory
\citep[Ch.~5]{vandervaart1998asymptotic} gives $\sqrt{n}\,(\hat\mu -
\bar R) \to \mathcal{N}(0, V)$ with $V = \operatorname{Var}(\psi(Z)/J -
Z)$, $J = \E[\psi'(Z)]$, so $\mu(\hat\theta) = \bar R$ holds in finite
samples up to $O_p(n^{-1/2})$. The population first-moment form
$\E_{\hat\theta,\kappa}[M] = m_{\mathrm{obs}}$, which the
differential-privacy sibling proves \citep{towell2026dpcoarsening}, is
the $n = 1$ case and the $n \to \infty$ limit of this statement; see
\cref{rem:loc-sketch}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Regression form of regime (A)]
\label{rem:glm}
The proof is written for reports from a single exponential family with a
common natural parameter. It extends verbatim to independent, non-identically
distributed reports with a unit-indexed natural parameter $\eta_i(\theta)$, a
generalized linear model: the gradient becomes $\sum_i
(\partial\eta_i/\partial\theta)^\top(\T(R_i) - m_i(\hat\theta)) = \bm 0$, the
aggregate score identity, and full column rank of the stacked Jacobian
$\partial\eta/\partial\theta$ plays the role of the rank hypothesis. The
spot-level (\cref{cor:spatial}) and bag-prevalence (\cref{cor:mil}) corollaries
are this regression form, where the exact statement is the weighted moment match
$(\partial\eta/\partial\theta)^\top(\bm Y - \hat{\bm p}) = \bm 0$ rather than a
coordinatewise $m(\hat\theta) = \bar\T$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The boundary of the location-family branch, characterized]
\label{rem:loc-sketch}
Regime (A) is an exact finite-sample identity: the exponential-family
mean-value identity holds at any interior optimum and the rank
hypothesis is the only ingredient. Regime (B)'s exact finite-sample
content is $m(\hat\theta) = \hat\mu$ with $\hat\mu$ the sample
$\psi$-location (the median for the Laplace kernel, the
$\tanh$-location for logistic, the sample mean for Gaussian). The
sample-mean form $\mu(\hat\theta) = \bar R$ is exactly characterized
\citep{teicher1961maximum}: the location MLE equals the arithmetic
sample mean for every sample if and only if the kernel is Gaussian. The
reason is short. Stationarity
of the log-likelihood at $\bar R$ for every sample is $\sum_i \psi(r_i)
= 0$ over the sum-zero residual hyperplane; specializing to $n = 3$
turns this into Cauchy's functional equation $\psi(a) + \psi(b) =
\psi(a + b)$, whose continuous (or monotone) solutions are linear,
forcing $\log p_0$ quadratic, that is, Gaussian. The classical face of
this is Gauss's characterization of the normal law as the unique
location family whose most probable value is the arithmetic mean, made
precise as the maximum-likelihood characterization of the normal by
\citet{teicher1961maximum} and placed in the broader characterization
theory of \citet{kagan1973characterization}. The
failure is therefore not an artifact and first appears at $n = 3$: on
data $(0, 1, 5)$ the Laplace MLE is the median $1$ against the mean
$2$, an exact gap of $-1$, and the logistic MLE sits at a strict,
smooth interior maximum a fixed distance from the mean, ruling out any
appeal to a kink in $\psi$. For $n \ge 3$ and a non-Gaussian kernel
the residual $\mu(\hat\theta) - \bar R$ is genuinely nonzero and of
order $n^{-1/2}$ (proof of the theorem), with limiting standard deviation
$\sqrt{V/n}$ for $V = \operatorname{Var}(\psi(Z)/J - Z)$: this constant, with
each kernel taken at unit scale parameter, is
$V = 1$ for the Laplace kernel (influence function $\operatorname{sign}(Z) - Z$),
$V \approx 0.290$ ($\sqrt{V} \approx 0.538$) for the logistic, and $V = 0$ for the
Gaussian, where the identity is exact and the branch collapses into regime (A); a
Monte Carlo check at large $n$ reproduces both the constants and the $n^{-1/2}$
slope. The population first-moment
identity $\E_{\hat\theta,\kappa}[M] = m_{\mathrm{obs}}$ of
\citet{towell2026dpcoarsening} is its $n = 1$ and limiting form.
Log-concavity (not mere unimodality) of $p_0$ is what makes $\hat\mu$ a
unique interior maximizer: a symmetric unimodal but non-log-concave
kernel such as Student-$t_2$ can give a bimodal likelihood with the
mean at a local minimum, so the identity is not even well posed. The
two regimes are not nested: (A) is a curved constraint on a discrete or
count report, (B) is a location shift of a continuous report, and the
differential-privacy identity is recovered only from (B).
\end{remark}

The single moral, stated once, is the warning each sibling issues:
because \eqref{eq:general-consistency} constrains only the mean of
$\T$, two parameter values that imply the same $m(\theta)$ but
different latent structure both satisfy it. Marginal-fit agreement is
therefore never evidence of unbiasedness; detecting bias requires the
singleton candidate sets of \cref{sec:identifiability} or external
information.

\subsection{The six named corollaries}

Each named consistency theorem is \cref{thm:general-consistency} read
in the coordinates of \cref{tab:css}. We state them compactly and flag
the two that do not reduce by regime (A) alone.

\begin{corollary}[Cell-total consistency, scRNA-seq]
\label{cor:scrna}
Take regime (A) with the zero-inflated negative-binomial report law
for gene $j$ and $\T(X) = X$. Then $m(\hat\theta) = (1 -
\hat\pi_j)\hat\mu_j$ and \eqref{eq:general-consistency} reads $(1 -
\hat\pi_j)\hat\mu_j = \bar X_j$. This is the cell-total consistency
theorem of \citet{towell2026scrnacoarsening}. The reduction is exact:
with the dispersion $\phi$ known, the ZINB
observed-count law is a regular exponential family whose natural
sufficient statistic is the pair $(X, \1\{X = 0\})$, the
negative-binomial base reweighted only at the zero cell, so the
regime-(A) mean-matching holds in both coordinates and cell-total
consistency is its $X$-coordinate. The $X$-moment continues to match
$\bar X_j$ even when $\phi$ is estimated jointly.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Spot-level consistency, spatial transcriptomics]
\label{cor:spatial}
Take regime (A) with the spot-level Poisson report law of mean $\sum_k
p_{s,k}\mu_{j,k}$ and $\T(X_{sj}) = X_{sj}$, both $\bm\mu_j$ and
$\bm p_s$ unconstrained at the optimum. Then
\eqref{eq:general-consistency} reads $\sum_k \hat P_{sk}\hat\mu_{j,k} =
\bar X_{sj}$, the spot-level consistency theorem of
\citet{towell2026spatialcoarsening}. The Poisson law is a regular
exponential family, so the per-coordinate identity is exact; the
\emph{vector} statement over $(s,j)$ additionally requires the joint
rank condition of \cref{thm:general-rank} so that the two score-kernel
conditions intersect in zero, which is the only place the rank
condition enters a consistency claim.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Code-frequency consistency, phenotyping]
\label{cor:phenotype}
Take regime (A) with the single-code Bernoulli report law and $\T(C) =
C$. The Bernoulli law depends on $(\pi, \mathrm{sens}, \mathrm{spec})$
only through the code frequency $q = \pi\,\mathrm{sens} +
(1-\pi)(1-\mathrm{spec})$, which is the natural mean parameter, and
\eqref{eq:general-consistency} reads $q(\hat\pi,
\widehat{\mathrm{sens}}, \widehat{\mathrm{spec}}) = \bar C$, the
code-frequency consistency theorem of
\citet{towell2026phenotypecoarsening}. The reduction is exact in the
informative regime $\mathrm{sens} + \mathrm{spec} > 1$, where the
gradient of $q$ in the parameters is nonzero, which is the
full-rank-Jacobian hypothesis of regime (A) specialized to the scalar
mean. The single-code likelihood depends on the three parameters
$(\pi, \mathrm{sens}, \mathrm{spec})$ only through the scalar $q$, so
consistency pins $q$ but not the triple: the $3 \to 1$ collapse is
exactly why the chart-review singleton of \cref{sec:identifiability} is
needed to recover the individual parameters.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Bag-prevalence consistency, multiple instance learning]
\label{cor:mil}
Take regime (A) with the bag-level Bernoulli report law under the
logical-OR aggregation rule, fitted bag-positive probability $\hat p_i =
1 - \exp(-\bm m_i^\top \hat{\bm s})$ for the bag-by-instance-type
composition row $\bm m_i$ and instance-type positivity parameter
$\bm s$, and $\T(Y) = Y$ the bag label. The regime-(A) stationarity
condition is the chain-ruled score $(\partial\eta/\partial\bm s)^\top(\bm
Y - \hat{\bm p}) = \bm 0$ from the proof of \cref{thm:general-consistency};
here the link Jacobian is $\partial\eta/\partial\bm s = D^{-1}M$, so it
reads $M^\top D^{-1}(\bm Y - \hat{\bm p}) = \bm 0$, with $M$ the
composition matrix and $D = \operatorname{diag}(\hat{\bm p})$: along every
direction in the column space of $M$ the fitted and observed bag-positive
counts coincide. This is the bag-prevalence consistency theorem of
\citet{towell2026milcoarsening}. The bag label is Bernoulli, a regular
exponential family, so the reduction is exact. The factor $D^{-1}$ is the
Jacobian of the non-canonical log-survival (noisy-OR) link: at the
canonical link $\partial\eta/\partial\bm s$ would be $M$ and the identity
would collapse to the unweighted $m(\hat\theta) = \bar\T$ of
\eqref{eq:general-consistency}, but the noisy-OR link carries the
inverse-fitted-rate weight, so the moment matching is IRLS-weighted. As in
spatial transcriptomics, the \emph{vector} statement over instance types
additionally requires the joint rank condition of \cref{thm:general-rank},
here full column rank of $M$, and the moral is the sibling's: bag-level
accuracy cannot validate instance-level scores.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Agreement consistency, weak supervision]
\label{cor:weaksup}
Take regime (A) with a label-model report law that is a regular
exponential family whose sufficient statistics \emph{include} the
pairwise agreement indicators $\T_{jk}(\bm\lambda) = \1\{\lambda_j =
\lambda_k\}$. Then \eqref{eq:general-consistency} reads
$\widehat{\Prob}\{\lambda_j = \lambda_k \mid \text{both vote}\} =
\bar A_{jk}$, the agreement consistency theorem of
\citet{towell2026weaksupcoarsening}. The reduction is exact only under
this sufficiency hypothesis. The standard naive-Bayes label model of
data programming \citep{ratner2016data} is parametrized by per-LF
accuracies and the class prior, for which the pairwise agreement
indicators are \emph{not} sufficient statistics; for that
parametrization the identity holds only asymptotically, at the
population parameter values and at the $n^{-1/2}$ rate, as established
in \citet{towell2026weaksupcoarsening}. This is the first seam: regime
(A) delivers agreement consistency exactly for a sufficiency-complete
parametrization and only in the limit for the parametrization
practitioners actually use.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Release consistency, differential privacy]
\label{cor:dp}
Take regime (B) with the additive-noise release $M = q(D) + Z$, $Z
\sim \kappa$, and $\T(M) = M$. This is the single-release case, $n = 1$:
the analyst observes one release $M$ and fits $\theta$ from the marginal
release density $p_M(m \mid \theta) = p_{\mathrm{conv}}(m -
\mu(\theta))$. For $p_{\mathrm{conv}}$ symmetric and unimodal its
log-density in $\theta$ is maximized where $m - \mu(\hat\theta) = 0$, so
$\mu(\hat\theta) = m$ exactly, with no appeal to differentiability or
to the kernel scale; because a symmetric unimodal density has mode equal
to mean, this is the exact first-moment identity $\E_{\hat\theta,\kappa}[M]
= m_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the release consistency theorem of
\citet{towell2026dpcoarsening}. The single release is what makes the
identity exact: it is the $n = 1$ instance of \cref{rem:loc-sketch}, and
it does not generalize to a sample-mean identity over several releases,
which would require the Gaussian kernel. This is the second seam: the
release law is a continuous convolution, not a curved exponential family
in $\theta$, so release consistency is recovered from the location-family
branch (B) and its proof uses the location-family score identity, not the
exponential-family mean-value identity that serves the other five
corollaries. With this reading the differential-privacy identity is an
instance of the same stationarity principle, but it is not a corollary
of regime (A).
\end{corollary}
