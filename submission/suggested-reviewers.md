# Suggested reviewers (coarsening-synthesis -> Statistical Science)

Proposed slate, for the author to confirm or edit before submitting. The paper is a
synthesis whose backbone is coarsening at random / missing-data identifiability, so
the strongest reviewers are experts in that foundation who also have the breadth to
judge a cross-domain claim. The editor decides; these are suggestions.

## Primary suggestions

1. **Mark J. van der Laan** (UC Berkeley). Co-author of the foundational
   coarsening-at-random framework (Gill, van der Laan and Robins 1997) and of
   targeted/semiparametric learning. The most natural authority on the paper's CAR
   backbone and on the semiparametric directions raised in the discussion.

2. **Daniel O. Scharfstein** (University of Miami). Missing-not-at-random
   sensitivity analysis and identification under incomplete data; well placed to
   judge the C2 boundary and the relation to the sensitivity-analysis lineage.

3. **Eric J. Tchetgen Tchetgen** (University of Pennsylvania, Wharton). Proximal
   causal inference, missing data, and semiparametric efficiency; the natural
   reviewer for the singleton-as-anchor device and the future-work directions.

4. **Thomas S. Richardson** (University of Washington). Identifiability and
   graphical models for incomplete data; strong on the rank-condition and
   identifiability half of the paper.

## Additional options (breadth)

5. **Roderick J. A. Little** (University of Michigan). Foundations of missing-data
   inference (MAR/MNAR, Little and Rubin); breadth to assess the cross-domain claim.

6. **Stijn Vansteelandt** (Ghent University). Missing data, measurement error, and
   semiparametrics; bridges the measurement-error / double-sampling lineage the
   paper cites.

If the editor wants the application sections refereed by domain experts, natural
additions are someone from the single-cell statistics community (for the scRNA-seq
and spatial corollaries) and someone from the weak-supervision / differential-
privacy side; the author can suggest these on request.

## Conflicts of interest

None known. The author (early-career, SIUE) has no recent co-authorship or advising
relationship with the suggested reviewers. None should be excluded on COI grounds
from the author's side.
